# Competitive Landscape for Lateralis: Attack Path / Exposure Management, Lateral Movement Detection, Agentic SOC, Open-Source Delivery Models (as of Nov 2026)

Research method: WebSearch and WebFetch only, plus the public GitHub REST API for repo license and star counts (queried 2026-11-21). No Firecrawl or Apollo. Vendor statements are marked "(vendor claim)". Where a search summary was the only access to a page, the claim is attributed to that page and was not read in full.

## 1. Attack path / exposure management: who does what, and does anyone forecast the next hop from live behaviour?

### Takeaway
Every major attack-path and exposure-management product found here builds its graph from **configuration and posture data**: identity relationships, permissions, vulnerabilities, cloud config, and reachability. It then ranks paths and "choke points" to critical assets. None of them publicly claims to **forecast the next lateral-movement hop from live authentication or network behaviour with calibrated probabilities**. The nearest commercial analogue is Akamai Hunt. Its 2023 blog describes temporal GNN link prediction on network-log snapshots, but uses it for anomaly flagging, not path forecasting, and publishes no accuracy figures. The academic prior art (Euler, Jbeil) is close to what Lateralis does.

- Microsoft's attack paths are generated "automatically" from collected data on devices, identities and cloud resources. Paths end at critical assets such as domain controllers, and choke points are nodes where several paths converge. This is a configuration and posture graph, not a behavioural forecast — [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-exposure-management/work-attack-paths-overview)
- XM Cyber research (vendor claim): "only 2% of attack paths lead to critical assets," and fixing choke points removes most of the risk — [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/most-attack-paths-are-dead-ends-but-2-lead-to-critical-assets-report/amp/)
- XM Cyber said it would release two open-source "exposure hunting engines" at Black Hat USA / DEF CON 2026. This signals that incumbents are also moving into open source — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/de/news-release/2026/03/17/3257259/0/en/xm-cyber-extends-its-continuous-exposure-management-platform-to-secure-ai-attack-surfaces.html)
- BloodHound CE and Enterprise share a codebase. CE has no SpecterOps support; Enterprise has dedicated support — [SpecterOps](https://specterops.io/platform/bloodhound-enterprise-vs-community-edition/)
- **Akamai Hunt** is the only commercial example found of temporal GNN link prediction for lateral movement. Its graphs come from periodic network-log snapshots, with node features such as role (DC, endpoint) and edge features such as ports, processes and the failed-connection ratio. GNNs are combined with RNN sequence encoders to "predict future links by assigning likelihood scores to potential edges". A new edge that "significantly deviates" from what the model expects is flagged as anomalous. **No accuracy, precision or false-positive figures were published**, and the example was a simulation — [Akamai blog, Oct 2023](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security/2023/oct/detect-and-remediate-attacks-with-akamai-hunt)
- Academic prior art: **Euler** (ACM TOPS 2023) treats lateral-movement detection as temporal-graph link prediction with a GNN plus a sequence encoder. **Jbeil** (2024) is an inductive temporal GNN for lateral-movement link prediction that works on unseen nodes, reporting AUC around 99% (author claim). Danneman & Hyde (2021) trained a feed-forward network to rank the host an adversary is likely to compromise next — [arXiv 2104.13195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13195); [IEEE S&P 2024 listing](https://computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/sp/2024/313000a009/1RjE9NF72De); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10540681)
- Gartner published its first Magic Quadrant for Exposure Assessment Platforms (EAP) on 10 Nov 2025. It covers the discovery, assessment, prioritization and mobilization stages of CTEM; Rapid7 and Tenable were Leaders and XM Cyber a Challenger (vendor-reported positions) — [Gartner abstract](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7159430); [Rapid7](https://rapid7.com/blog/post/em-rapid7-leader-2025-gartner-exposure-assessment-platform-magic-quadrant-mq-eap); [Tenable](https://tenable.com/analyst-research/tenable-gartner-magic-quadrant-exposure-assessment-platforms)
- Gartner Market Guide for Adversarial Exposure Validation, 24 Mar 2026. Gartner reportedly expects EAP and AEV to converge into a "Unified Exposure Management Platform". ArmorCode quotes Gartner predicting UEMPs will grow from under 5% to 60% of the market by 2028; this figure is unverified — [Picus page hosting the Market Guide](https://www.picussecurity.com/test-27/report/2026-gartner-market-guide-for-automated-exposure-validation?hs_amp=true); [XM Cyber Gartner summit recap](https://xmcyber.com/blog/initial-reactions-and-key-takeaways-from-the-2026-gartner-security-and-risk-summit/); [ArmorCode](https://www.armorcode.com/report/gartner-how-to-secure-enterprise-agentic-ai-ambition)

### Inferences
- The incumbents answer the question "what paths exist?" (static reachability). Lateralis can answer "which hop is the attacker most likely to take next, given what is happening right now?" (behavioural, temporal). The two are complementary. A credible positioning is "the behavioural layer on top of a BloodHound-style static graph", not "a replacement for XM Cyber".
- Because Euler, Jbeil and Akamai exist, Lateralis cannot claim temporal GNN link prediction for lateral movement as a new idea. It can claim it as an **open, self-hosted, productized implementation** that adds path ranking to critical assets, calibrated probabilities, and cost-aware defense recommendation. None of the sources above publish these together.
- XM Cyber's "choke point" and Microsoft's "choke point" concepts are the established vocabulary for "cheapest effective defense". Lateralis should use that term and say how it differs: choke points weighted by forecast probability and remediation cost, rather than by static path count.

### Gaps
- Orca Security's attack-path features and pricing were not covered in results. Palo Alto Cortex Exposure Management / Cortex Cloud attack paths were not researched in depth.
- No public list pricing was found for BloodHound Enterprise, XM Cyber, Wiz, CrowdStrike FEM, Pentera, NodeZero, SafeBreach or AttackIQ.
- Akamai Hunt's current (2026) product claims were not checked. Only the 2023 blog was read.
- SafeBreach and AttackIQ on-prem options were not independently confirmed.

## 2. Lateral movement detection specifically: approaches and claims

### Takeaway
Lateral-movement vendors fall into three camps. **Detection-led behavioural AI** (Vectra, Darktrace) flags lateral movement after it happens. **Prevention-led microsegmentation or identity enforcement** (Zero Networks, Illumio, Silverfort) removes or gates paths. **Identity sensors** (Microsoft Defender for Identity) are the third. None of the sources found publish a forward-looking "next hop" forecast. Microsoft Defender for Identity's own Lateral Movement Paths feature lost its data source (SAM-R collection) in May 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Vectra AI** (vendor claim): detects "reconnaissance, lateral movement, and command-and-control" across network, AD, Entra ID and SaaS. Vectra also claims Darktrace has "limited identity and SaaS-specific attack detection" (competitor claim) — [Vectra lateral movement use case](https://www.vectra.ai/solutions/use-cases/lateral-movement-detection-and-containment); [Vectra vs Darktrace](https://it.vectra.ai/products/competitive/darktrace)
- **Darktrace** (vendor case write-ups): anomaly "model breaches" such as "Device / Multiple Lateral Movement Model Breaches". In a Citrix Bleed case it detected reconnaissance and lateral movement days after initial beaconing — [Darktrace Citrix Bleed blog](https://darktrace.com/blog/stemming-the-citrix-bleed-vulnerability-with-darktraces-activeai-platform); [Darktrace crypto-botnet blog](https://darktrace.com/es/blog/crypto-botnets-moving-laterally)
- **Zero Networks** (vendor position): "the solution isn't faster detection... it's preventing lateral movement in the first place with modern microsegmentation". Its automation learns allowed behaviours to create dynamic rules, including for service accounts — [Zero Networks guide](https://zeronetworks.com/blog/best-tools-for-lateral-threat-prevention-microsegmentation-feature-guide)
- **Silverfort** (founder thought leadership): MFA is typically not applied to internal assets, so stopping lateral movement needs identity-threat visibility plus internal MFA enforcement — [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/lateral-movement-a-crash-course)
- **Microsoft Defender for Identity**: remote SAM-R collection of local-admin group membership was disabled from early May 2025, so "the data used to calculate potential lateral movement paths will no longer be collected" — [Message Center MC1073068 via merill.net](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1073068); [MS Learn SAM-R page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-for-identity/deploy/remote-calls-sam). A Microsoft training lab says LMPs are deprecated and points to Defender XDR attack paths instead, while the main Learn LMP page still describes the feature. Sources conflict on formal status — [MS training lab](https://microsoft.github.io/TWL300-XDR-SOC-Operations/docs/Ex04/04_02.html); [MS Learn LMP](https://learn.microsoft.com/defender-for-identity/understand-lateral-movement-paths)

### Inferences
- Detection-led vendors are reactive. Prevention vendors need policy change and a microsegmentation rollout. A forecast of the next hop with a calibrated probability sits between them: it tells the SOC where to watch, and the segmentation or identity team which single rule to add first. That framing fits "cheapest effective defense".
- Lateralis can integrate rather than compete. For example, it can output a recommended block or MFA rule in a format a Zero Networks, Illumio or Silverfort-style control could consume, or that Wazuh active-response could run.
- The SAM-R change shows that the static data needed for "who is local admin where" is getting harder to collect. Behavioural signals (auth logs) are an alternative input.

### Gaps
- Illumio, ExtraHop and Exabeam/UEBA claims were not retrieved in this pass. Silverfort's current product documentation was not read.
- No independent head-to-head evaluation of lateral-movement detection accuracy across vendors was found.

## 3. Agentic SOC (2024–2026): what the agents do, how human approval works, and the evidence for their claims

### Takeaway
By 2026 every platform vendor ships triage and investigation agents: Microsoft, Google, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto and SentinelOne. Response actions are almost universally gated by **admin-defined boundaries plus human-in-the-loop approval for high-impact actions**. All headline performance numbers are vendor-reported. Gartner moved "AI SOC agents" to the Peak of Inflated Expectations in its June 2026 Security Operations Hype Cycle, with only 1–5% market penetration. Venture funding into AI SOC startups (Torq, 7AI, Exaforce, Dropzone, Prophet) is large, and every one of those products is SaaS.

### Inferences
- "Triage agent + response with approval + copilot chat" is now a **commodity feature set** across incumbents and open-source projects such as AiSOC. Lateralis's four agents alone cannot be the differentiator. What makes them distinct is that they are **grounded in the forecast**: the triage agent explains *why* a hop is likely, the response agent proposes the specific choke-point fix, and the red-team agent validates the forecast in a lab.
- Approval UX norms to match: per-action risk tiers, RBAC inheritance, explicit "no autonomous response by default" (CrowdStrike), and a full audit ledger (AiSOC, AgentiX). The minimum credible Lateralis design is approval-by-default for every containment action, plus an immutable action log.
- All the commercial agentic SOC products found are SaaS and send telemetry to vendor-hosted LLMs. "Self-hosted with local or pseudonymized LLM calls" is a real gap among commercial products, but **not among open-source ones**, since AiSOC already offers local LLMs.
- Gartner's "Peak of Inflated Expectations" framing argues for publishing honest evaluation numbers, such as forecast calibration and triage agreement with analysts. That counters "AI washing" scepticism.

### Gaps
- Not covered with sources in this pass: Sec-Gemini, 7AI's product mechanics, Prophet Security, Intezer, Radiant Security, Conifers, Exaforce's product details, and Tines AI.
- No independent (non-vendor) benchmark of AI SOC agent accuracy was found.

## 4. Open-source counterparts: how they ship, license, and expose APIs (open-core models)

### Takeaway
The standard self-hosted security pattern is **Docker Compose (sometimes plus Helm/K8s) + a REST API with token auth + a permissive or copyleft community license, with paid features gated by a license key in the same codebase** (open core). In recent years the licenses have drifted toward copyleft or source-available terms to block SaaS free-riding: AGPL (Shuffle, Tracecat, Wazuh engine), Elastic License 2.0 (Security Onion), and proprietary freemium (TheHive 5).

### Inferences
- The model closest to Lateralis is **BloodHound CE/Enterprise**: one codebase, a free self-hosted Docker Compose community edition, paid enterprise features plus support, and a well-documented API with HMAC-signed tokens. Copying its API auth design (token ID + secret, HMAC over method, URI, date and body) is low-risk and credible.
- License choice:
  - **Apache-2.0** maximizes adoption and enterprise comfort (BloodHound CE, OpenCTI CE, Caldera) but allows SaaS free-riding.
  - **AGPL-3.0** (Shuffle, Tracecat, Wazuh engine) protects against cloud resellers while staying OSI-approved.
  - **ELv2 / proprietary freemium** (Security Onion, TheHive 5) are not open source and could undercut an "open-source" claim.
  - For a student project seeking community trust plus a future open-core path, **AGPL-3.0 core + commercial EE license key in an `/ee` directory** (the Tracecat/OpenCTI pattern) is the most common recent choice.
- The TheHive 5 relicensing (archived AGPL repo, 2-user limit) is a cautionary tale. Abrupt license tightening damages community trust, so the license should be set up front.
- Packaging norms to match: `docker compose up -d` with a `.env.example`, bind to localhost by default, documented RAM minimum, Helm chart later, and a sample/demo dataset.

## 5. Market context: exposure management and AI SOC market sizes, and the Indian regulatory context

### Takeaway
Market-research firms put exposure management at roughly $2B (2024), growing about 27–28% a year to $7.6–9.8B by 2029–2030. They size "AI SOC" at about $15B (2025) and $47B by 2031 (21.1% CAGR). These are single-vendor paid reports with broad definitions, so treat them as directional. In India, the CERT-In 2022 Directions require **6-hour incident reporting** and **180-day log retention**. The DPDP Rules were notified **14 Nov 2025** with an 18-month phased rollout. Penalties for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards reach **₹250 crore**. Cross-border transfer (Section 16) uses a "negative list" that is still empty and is expected to take effect around May 2027. India's average breach lifecycle (identify + contain) was **263 days** in IBM's 2025 report.

### Inferences
- Authentication and network logs (usernames, hostnames, IPs) are personal data under DPDP. Sending them raw to a foreign-hosted LLM API makes the customer a Data Fiduciary transferring personal data abroad, which is permitted today but exposed to future negative-list notifications and sectoral localisation rules. It also creates "reasonable security safeguards" exposure (₹250 crore ceiling). A **self-hosted model with local LLMs, or with deterministic pseudonymization of identifiers before any external LLM call**, is therefore a concrete compliance selling point for Indian BFSI, healthcare and government buyers. (This is my inference, not legal advice; the sources do not discuss LLMs specifically.)
- CERT-In's 180-day retention means Indian enterprises already keep the auth and network logs Lateralis needs, which lowers onboarding friction. The 6-hour reporting window supports the value of fast, explained triage.
- The 263-day lifecycle and IBM's automation data support a "reduce time to identify lateral movement" pitch. Those figures measure identify-plus-contain time, which is not the same as attacker dwell time.

## 6. Which gaps and differentiators can Lateralis credibly claim?

### Takeaway
Credible: (1) **behavioural, temporal next-hop forecasting with calibrated probabilities** on top of a static path view; (2) **cost-aware choke-point recommendation** weighted by forecast likelihood; (3) **self-hosted, open-source, with local or pseudonymized LLM agents** that are grounded in the forecast and gated by human approval; (4) a **closed loop** in which the red-team agent tests forecasts in a Docker lab. Not credible: "first GNN for lateral movement" (Euler, Jbeil and Akamai precede it), "first open-source AI SOC" (AiSOC, Vigil), or a claim to replace the full EAP or XDR platforms.

### Cited Findings
- Incumbent attack-path graphs are generated from collected configuration and asset data (Microsoft), identity and permission graphs (BloodHound), or attack-graph analysis of exposures (XM Cyber). None of these sources claim forecasting from live behaviour — [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-exposure-management/work-attack-paths-overview); [SpecterOps](https://specterops.io/platform/bloodhound-enterprise-vs-community-edition/); [XM brochure](https://xmcyber.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/XM-Company-Brochure.pdf)
- The temporal GNN link-prediction approach exists in research and in one vendor blog (Akamai Hunt), which publishes no accuracy figures — [Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security/2023/oct/detect-and-remediate-attacks-with-akamai-hunt); [arXiv 2104.13195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13195); [NSF PAR (Jbeil)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10540681)
- All the commercial agentic SOC products found are SaaS. Human approval for high-impact actions is the industry norm — [PANW](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/company/press/2025/palo-alto-networks-unveils-cortex-agentix-to-build--deploy-and-govern-the-agentic-workforce-of-the-future); [CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/platform/charlotte-ai/); [SentinelOne](https://www.sentinelone.com/it/press/sentinelone-makes-the-autonomous-soc-trustworthy-with-governed-closed-loop-response/)
- An open-source, self-hosted AI SOC with local LLMs already exists (AiSOC, MIT, ~7.1k stars) — [AiSOC README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beenuar/AiSOC/main/README.md)
- Gartner expects EAP and AEV (validation) to converge. A product that both predicts paths and validates them (the red-team lab) maps onto that direction — [XM Cyber Gartner recap](https://xmcyber.com/blog/initial-reactions-and-key-takeaways-from-the-2026-gartner-security-and-risk-summit/)
- XM Cyber is releasing open-source exposure-hunting engines (2026), so incumbents are entering open source too — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/de/news-release/2026/03/17/3257259/0/en/xm-cyber-extends-its-continuous-exposure-management-platform-to-secure-ai-attack-surfaces.html)

### Inferences
- **Positioning sentence (draft):** "BloodHound shows the paths that exist. Lateralis forecasts the one an attacker is most likely to take next, from your live auth and network logs, with calibrated probabilities. It then recommends the cheapest control that cuts the most probable paths to your crown jewels. Self-hosted, open source, and its AI agents never send raw identities off-box."
- **Differentiators ranked by credibility:**
  1. *Calibrated next-hop probabilities with published reliability diagrams and Brier/ECE on public datasets* (e.g. LANL). None of the vendors or the Akamai blog publish calibration, so this is easy to prove and hard for SaaS incumbents to match publicly.
  2. *Forecast-weighted choke points + cost model.* This extends the established choke-point concept with probability × remediation cost.
  3. *Data sovereignty:* self-hosted, local LLM option, deterministic pseudonymization before any external LLM, aimed at DPDP/CERT-In buyers.
  4. *Agents grounded in model output* rather than generic triage. The red-team lab agent provides empirical validation of forecasts, in line with the EAP/AEV convergence.
  5. *Open source + documented API* (BloodHound-style HMAC tokens) that complements, rather than replaces, BloodHound CE, Wazuh and Security Onion. It ingests their data.
- **Realistic B2B delivery model, following the comparable projects:**
  - Community edition: AGPL-3.0 (or Apache-2.0 if adoption matters more than protection), shipped as `docker compose up` with a demo dataset, binding to localhost by default.
  - Enterprise key (OpenCTI/Tracecat pattern) unlocking SSO/RBAC, multi-tenant, long-horizon retention, priority model updates and support.
  - Later, an MSSP/partner channel for the Indian mid-market.
  - Avoid TheHive-style post-hoc restrictions.
- **Claims to avoid:** "first", "autonomous SOC", or any accuracy figure not from a reproducible public benchmark.
- **Main risk:** the core GNN competes with research code and with Akamai. The defensible value is the productization (calibration, cost model, agents, packaging), not the model architecture.
